# Decisions that transfer to other films

## Evidence before impact

Read the primary study's methods, tables, and outcome definitions before extracting a claim. Record the sample, comparator, duration, scale, before/after values, and calculation. Distinguish performance, preference, and participant counts. A study of another system is not validation of the advertised product.

Lanclo example: native-model mean scores 210 → 275.52; self-voice model 213.48 → 300.09. Gains are 65.52 and 86.61; their ratio is about 1.3. This does not establish 130% learning efficiency. See the film's research credits for the citation. Do not reuse these numbers in unrelated projects.

A useful data scene pairs a short, accurate headline with an interpretable graph. Use a common zero baseline for bar lengths, label the comparison, emphasize the relevant difference, and retain readable source/context notes. Removing excess decimals is different from changing the measured outcome.

## Replace a feature label with an experience

Instead of only “AI analyzes your weaknesses,” show:

1. The learner speaks.
2. A radar highlights the relevant area.
3. That result leads to a personalized set of questions.
4. The learner reads the next sentence aloud.

Confirm the real product behavior first. Candidate selection is not the same as generating new sentences. Example scores, questions, and voices should be identified as demonstrations where needed.

## Connect scenes through the user's routine

A news feature can feel disconnected after pronunciation analysis. A bridge such as “Your daily news becomes your English learning material” explains why it belongs before showing overnight collection, interest-based selection, and morning delivery.

The principle is reusable; the news feature is specific to Lanclo. Other projects need their own connection.

## Convert feedback into a narrow edit

- “Too repetitive”: vary relevant characters or situations, keeping the illustration family.
- “I cannot tell where to look”: simplify labels and animate the actual comparison, rather than decorating a number.
- “The scene feels static”: choose a meaningful sequence of action and response before adding effects.
- “That sounds like another person”: inspect voice, source take, delivery, and processing before changing the whole soundtrack.

Record the accepted change and preserve unrelated decisions. If the feedback is ambiguous, propose the smallest concrete interpretation.

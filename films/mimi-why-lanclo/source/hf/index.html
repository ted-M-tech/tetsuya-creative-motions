At a discussion abroad, I was the only one left out.
Everyone else was talking like crazy.
In Japan, I studied English with vocabulary, grammar and silent reading.
I could handle the conversations I knew.
But all I did was repeat them.
The people I met abroad spoke with confidence.
Their grammar was all over the place, but they gave their opinions boldly.
Even if reading was hard for them.
The opposite of me, a Japanese learner. (Me: reading ◎ speaking △ / Them: reading △ speaking ◎)
What the real world needs is the power to think for yourself, speak and discuss. (Meetings · Debates · Small talk)
Lanclo co-developer & advisor, a university lecturer who speaks five languages, says:
“Think for yourself, speak, and fix it. And keep repeating that.”
Think → Speak → Fix, again and again.
When you try to speak, you notice what you can’t say yet. (The Output Hypothesis in second language acquisition research)
That’s why Lanclo, on purpose, has features that need your voice, and features you can’t do without thinking.
Lay out the pieces of English learning… (Features designed under the lecturer’s supervision)
1. Daily lesson: pronunciation and reading aloud
2. Look at a photo and speak for up to 3 minutes
3. English journal: talk about your day, then fix it
4. Conversation practice: listen and answer on the spot
5. Debate: your opinion and a reason
6. Phrase review: a quick swipe
Input too, with a quick swipe. But our focus is thinking and speaking out loud. (Grammar, reading: you can do those at a desk.)
Practising at a desk is easier. I get it.
But when you think and speak for yourself, you start to see weak spots you couldn’t see before. (No reason comes out · The past tense won’t come quickly · Always the same phrases)
It’s hard, but I’ve built a system that supports you naturally.
